# Duel test — Deadpan Forecasts for Cities That Do Not Exist

Six candidate forecast lines were duelled pairwise in both directions
under the kit judge brief (proxy judging, dry deadpan TV meteorologist:
on-subject forecast first, then funnier joke, then more vivid detail,
then believable broadcast tone, then character use).

## Result

- Winner: the Brindlewick line — mean share 0.74
- Runner-up: the Marrowgate line — mean share 0.62
- The Brindlewick line clears the 0.60 bar and was submitted unchanged.

## Critic objections and answers

The rival critic raised five objections against the winning line. Each
was answered with no change to the line:

1. **One-trick reversal.** The line is a single reversal (rain falling
   upwards). Answer: the second beat is not another trick but an
   instruction — residents are told to hold their umbrellas upside
   down — which turns the reversal into a broadcast, not a punchline.
2. **Three time markers.** Noon, 3 p.m., and 6 p.m. is a lot of clock
   detail. Answer: the times are broadcast texture; a forecast that
   names its hours sounds like a forecast.
3. **Blunt twist.** The upward rain is stated plainly rather than
   built up to. Answer: stating it plainly is what makes it deadpan;
   a wink would break the tone.
4. **Too-plausible city name.** Brindlewick sounds like a real place.
   Answer: believable is the point — the city has to feel real for
   the forecast to land.
5. **Four unused characters.** The line is 156 of the 160 allowed
   characters. Answer: 97.5% of the budget is used; padding it to
   exactly 160 would add filler, not value.
